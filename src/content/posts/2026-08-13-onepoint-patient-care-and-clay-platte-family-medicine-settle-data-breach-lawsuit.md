A recent data breach settlement in the United States has significant implications for Australian healthcare providers, highlighting the importance of robust cybersecurity measures to protect sensitive patient information. OnePoint Patient Care, a Kentucky-based hospice-dedicated pharmacy, has agreed to a $2,115,000 settlement following a 2024 data breach that exposed the protected health information of over 1.7 million individuals. The breach occurred when hackers gained access to the company's systems, copying files between August 6 and August 8, 2024. The lawsuit alleged that OnePoint Patient Care failed to implement appropriate cybersecurity measures, leaving its systems vulnerable to attack. This settlement serves as a reminder to Australian healthcare organisations of their obligations under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, particularly in relation to the protection of sensitive health information. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires organisations to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. In light of this settlement, Australian healthcare providers should review their cybersecurity measures to ensure they are aligned with the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance, which provide a framework for protecting against cyber threats. Implementing robust security measures, such as multi-factor authentication and regular software updates, can help prevent similar breaches and reduce the risk of reputational damage and financial losses. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure (SOCI) Act 2018 also imposes obligations on certain healthcare organisations to implement cybersecurity measures to protect critical infrastructure. The settlement also highlights the potential consequences of failing to implement adequate cybersecurity measures, including financial penalties and reputational damage. As such, Australian healthcare providers must prioritise cybersecurity and ensure they have adequate measures in place to protect sensitive patient information. By doing so, they can reduce the risk of data breaches and maintain the trust of their patients. The settlement is a timely reminder for Australian healthcare organisations to review their cybersecurity posture and ensure they are compliant with relevant regulations and guidelines. Australian healthcare providers should take note of the settlement and take proactive steps to enhance their cybersecurity measures to prevent similar breaches from occurring. This includes regularly reviewing and updating their cybersecurity policies and procedures, providing training to staff on cybersecurity best practices, and implementing robust security measures to protect sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multi-factor authentication to prevent unauthorised access to systems and data
- Regularly update software and systems to ensure they are protected against known vulnerabilities
- Conduct regular cybersecurity risk assessments to identify and mitigate potential threats
