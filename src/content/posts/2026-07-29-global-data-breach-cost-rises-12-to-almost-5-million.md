A recent study by IBM has found that the average cost of a data breach has risen by 12% to almost $5 million, with the healthcare sector facing the highest breach costs at an average of $6.64 million per incident. Although this represents a 10.5% decrease from the previous year, it still underscores the significant financial risks associated with data breaches in the healthcare industry. For Australian healthcare providers, this serves as a critical reminder of the importance of robust cybersecurity measures, particularly in light of the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB) scheme also necessitates that healthcare organisations have protocols in place for identifying and reporting data breaches in a timely manner. The study highlighted that malicious or criminal attacks were the most common cause of breaches in healthcare, accounting for 59% of incidents, followed by IT failures and human error. The increasing use of AI tools by attackers to scan for vulnerabilities, craft phishing lures, and automate attacks at scale poses a new challenge. Australian healthcare providers must stay vigilant and consider the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance to bolster their cybersecurity defenses. The rise in AI-driven attacks, which have an increased financial impact, adding around $1 million to average data breach costs, is particularly concerning. Moreover, the lack of governance policies to mitigate or manage the risk to AI, with only around one third of organisations having a strict approval for deploying AI tools, is an area that needs immediate attention. The implications of these findings for Australian healthcare organisations are profound, suggesting a need for enhanced cybersecurity measures, better governance of AI tools, and adherence to the Security of Critical Infrastructure (SOCI) Act where applicable. By prioritising these aspects, healthcare providers can reduce their vulnerability to data breaches and the associated financial and reputational damages.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multifactor authentication to reduce the risk of breaches due to malicious or criminal attacks, as recommended by the ASD Essential Eight
- Conduct regular cybersecurity audits and risk assessments to identify vulnerabilities, particularly in relation to the use of AI tools, and ensure compliance with the ACSC guidance
- Develop and enforce strict governance policies for the deployment and use of AI tools within the organisation to mitigate potential risks and align with the SOCI Act
