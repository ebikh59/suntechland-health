A recent settlement has resolved class action data breach lawsuits against two US healthcare providers, McKenzie Health System and Aspire Health Alliance. The settlements were agreed to avoid further litigation costs and expenses. McKenzie Health System, a critical access hospital in Michigan, experienced a cyberattack and data breach in April 2025, resulting in the potential compromise of patient information, including names, addresses, and medical record numbers. The breach was reported to the US Department of Health and Human Services' Office for Civil Rights as affecting 58,839 individuals. In Australia, healthcare providers must comply with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, which require entities to take reasonable steps to protect personal information from unauthorised access, disclosure, or breach. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires entities to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. The settlements highlight the importance of implementing robust cybersecurity measures to prevent data breaches, such as those outlined in the ASD Essential Eight. Australian healthcare providers should review their cybersecurity controls and ensure they are aligned with ACSC guidance and the SOCI Act to prevent similar incidents. The settlements also provide a reminder of the potential consequences of a data breach, including reputational damage, financial losses, and regulatory action. By prioritising cybersecurity and complying with relevant regulations, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of a data breach and protect sensitive patient information. The settlements serve as a reminder for Australian healthcare providers to review their incident response plans and ensure they are prepared to respond quickly and effectively in the event of a data breach. This includes having procedures in place for notifying affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, as well as providing support to individuals who may have been affected by the breach.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multi-factor authentication to prevent unauthorised access to systems and data
- Regularly update and patch software and systems to prevent exploitation of known vulnerabilities
- Conduct regular cybersecurity awareness training for staff to prevent phishing and other social engineering attacks
