## What happened

Bradford Health Services, LLC and Bradford Health Partners, LLC, US healthcare providers, have reached a settlement in class action litigation over a December 2023 cybersecurity incident.

According to HIPAA Journal, unauthorised access was detected on 8 December 2023. "The data review was not completed until May 2025, and notification letters started to be mailed later that month", 18 months after detection. The lawsuit says 32,425 people were affected, while the breach report to the HHS Office for Civil Rights lists 28,543.

The compromised information included names, dates of birth, driver's licence numbers, medical diagnosis and treatment information, health insurance information, financial account numbers, passport numbers, payment card numbers, access credentials and Social Security numbers. The Hunters International ransomware group claimed responsibility and said it exfiltrated more than 760GB of data.

The consolidated case, In re Bradford Health Services, LLC Data Breach Litigation, was filed in the Circuit Court of Jefferson County, Alabama, with claims including negligence, negligence per se, breach of contract and unjust enrichment. Following mediation in October 2025, the settlement received preliminary approval. Class members can receive three years of medical data monitoring and claim up to US$5,000 in documented losses, or an alternative cash payment estimated at US$150. The claims deadline was 17 August 2026, and the final fairness hearing was set for 1 September 2026.

## Technical and operational significance

The most striking feature is the notification delay. Eighteen months passed between detection and notification while the organisation reviewed the stolen data to identify who was affected and what information was involved. During that time, data that a ransomware group said it had stolen was potentially available to criminals, while the people concerned did not know.

Long reviews usually reflect the difficulty of analysing large volumes of unstructured files such as documents, spreadsheets and scanned forms. Organisations that do not know where sensitive data is held before an incident face a slow, expensive reconstruction afterwards.

The data involved is especially sensitive. Diagnosis and treatment details combined with identity documents, financial and payment card data and access credentials give criminals what they need for both fraud and targeted extortion.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

Under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, Australian organisations must take reasonable steps to complete an assessment of a suspected eligible breach within 30 days, and then notify affected individuals and the OAIC as soon as practicable. An 18-month delay would be difficult to justify.

Australian alcohol and other drug services, mental health providers and sexual health clinics hold information whose exposure is particularly harmful. These services should prioritise data minimisation, strong access controls and pre-incident data mapping so that they can assess and notify quickly.

## What to watch next

- Final approval of the settlement.
- Continued enforcement attention to notification delays in the US.
- Activity by Hunters International and successor groups against healthcare.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Map where sensitive patient data is stored, including unstructured files, so a breach can be scoped within Notifiable Data Breaches timeframes.
- Arrange data breach review support, such as forensic and e-discovery providers, before an incident occurs.
- Apply extra protections to highly sensitive records, such as AOD, mental health and sexual health, including restricted access and encryption.
- Minimise retention of identity documents, payment card data and passport numbers that are not needed for care.
- Monitor for and rapidly contain ransomware precursors, such as credential theft and large outbound data transfers.
