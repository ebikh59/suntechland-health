A recent report has found that adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) phishing has become the most common method used by attackers to gain initial access to law firms, surpassing traditional credential theft. This shift is particularly notable given the widespread adoption of multifactor authentication (MFA) in the legal sector. According to the report, AiTM attacks accounted for 28.57% of all initial access events in the legal sector, with a 20% year-over-year increase in incidents targeting legal organisations. The report suggests that attackers have adapted to the use of MFA by using AiTM attacks, which can bypass MFA by proxying the authentication process. This has significant implications for Australian healthcare providers, who must ensure they are using phishing-resistant MFA and monitoring identity platform logs for anomalous session activity. The report's findings also highlight the importance of having a formal incident response plan in place, with only 34% of law firms reportedly having such a plan. Australian healthcare providers must review their cybersecurity measures to ensure compliance with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, as well as the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. The use of AiTM phishing attacks also underscores the need for healthcare providers to follow the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance to prevent and respond to cyber threats. Furthermore, the SOCI Act requires certain organisations to report cyber incidents, and healthcare providers must be aware of their obligations under this act. By prioritising cybersecurity and implementing robust measures to prevent and respond to attacks, Australian healthcare providers can protect sensitive patient information and maintain trust in the healthcare system.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement phishing-resistant MFA such as FIDO2 keys and passkeys to prevent AiTM phishing attacks
- Adopt conditional access policies that evaluate device health and location to reduce the risk of initial access events
- Monitor identity platform logs for anomalous session activity to quickly detect and respond to potential security incidents
