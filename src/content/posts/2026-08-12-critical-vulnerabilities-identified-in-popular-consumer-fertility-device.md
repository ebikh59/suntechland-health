A recent security assessment has identified critical vulnerabilities in the Mira Hormone Monitor, a popular consumer fertility tracking device, and its associated Android app. The vulnerabilities, which include weak or missing authentication and hard-coded API keys, could be exploited by an attacker to gain read and write access to reproductive health profiles, resulting in forgery, deletion, or destruction of health information. This could have significant implications for Australian healthcare providers, who must ensure the security and integrity of sensitive patient data in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires healthcare providers to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight provides a framework for mitigating cyber threats, including the implementation of application whitelisting and patching of operating systems and applications. In this case, the vulnerabilities in the Mira Hormone Monitor and its associated Android app highlight the importance of regular security assessments and patching of medical devices and software. Australian healthcare IT and security teams should be aware of these vulnerabilities and take steps to mitigate them, including implementing robust authentication and authorisation mechanisms and regularly updating software and firmware. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act also highlights the need for healthcare providers to ensure the security and resilience of their systems and data. By prioritising cybersecurity and implementing robust security measures, Australian healthcare providers can protect sensitive patient data and prevent potential harm to individuals and the healthcare system as a whole. The identification of these vulnerabilities also underscores the importance of collaboration between healthcare providers, medical device manufacturers, and cybersecurity experts to identify and address potential security risks. Ultimately, the security of medical devices and software is a shared responsibility, and all stakeholders must work together to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of sensitive patient data.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement robust authentication and authorisation mechanisms for all medical devices and software
- Regularly update software and firmware to patch known vulnerabilities
- Conduct regular security assessments and risk analyses to identify potential security threats
