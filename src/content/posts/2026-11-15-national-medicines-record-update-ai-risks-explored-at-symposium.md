## What happened

The Department of Health, Disability and Ageing is continuing work towards a single national view of patients' medicines across healthcare settings. The National Medicines Record is set to be rolled out progressively through to the end of 2027, Pulse+IT reported.

The update was given at the Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care's National Medicines Symposium 2026. The symposium, held with the theme of medicines stewardship, was livestreamed on 11 November 2026. Its program included a session on digital health tools for medicines stewardship featuring Daniel McCabe, First Assistant Secretary of the department's Technology Assessment and Access Division. According to Pulse+IT's headline, AI risks were also explored at the symposium.

The National Medicines Record has been described as an overarching prescription and medicines record linked to a patient's My Health Record.

*Pulse+IT's full article is behind a subscriber paywall. This brief is limited to its public summary and the published symposium program; the details of the AI risk discussion are not publicly available.*

## Technical and operational significance

A single national record of a patient's medicines has clear safety benefits. It can reduce medication errors at transitions of care, such as hospital admission and discharge, and give clinicians a more complete picture of what patients are taking.

It also creates a significant, centralised dataset of highly sensitive information. Medication histories can reveal conditions including mental illness, HIV, substance use and reproductive health. A national record therefore needs strong access controls, audit logging, protection against misuse by authorised users, and resilience against outages that could affect prescribing.

Integration matters too. The record will draw on and feed into prescribing, dispensing and clinical systems across many providers, each of which is a potential point of compromise.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

Health services, pharmacies and general practices connecting to the National Medicines Record should prepare for the security and privacy obligations that come with access. These include managing user accounts carefully, monitoring access, and making sure clinical software meets national conformance and security requirements.

If AI tools are used to analyse or summarise medication records, for example to flag interactions or reconcile medicines, governance, validation and data handling arrangements need to be clear.

## What to watch next

- Detailed rollout milestones and conformance requirements for connecting systems.
- Security and access control design for the national record.
- Guidance on AI use with national medicines data.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Review how your organisation manages user access to My Health Record and plan to apply the same rigour to the National Medicines Record.
- Make sure clinical and dispensing software is kept current and meets national conformance and security requirements.
- Monitor and audit access to medication histories, which can reveal highly sensitive conditions.
- Establish downtime procedures for medication reconciliation in case national systems are unavailable.
- Assess governance and validation for any AI tools that will process medication records.
