A recent multidistrict litigation case in the US has highlighted the importance of robust data protection measures in the healthcare sector. The case involves a massive ransomware attack on Change Healthcare in 2024, which resulted in the theft of approximately 6 terabytes of sensitive data, including electronic protected health information of around 192,700,000 individuals. The stolen data includes names, contact information, Social Security numbers, driver’s license numbers, insurance information, and medical information. To protect this sensitive data, strict rules have been established for its handling by attorneys involved in the lawsuit. These rules dictate that the data must be stored on an encrypted hard drive, with the decryption key provided separately, and that the data must be encrypted again by the plaintiffs’ attorneys using industry-standard encryption once they receive the hard drive. The rules also prohibit the creation of copies of the data and its storage in shared file libraries. For Australian healthcare providers, this case serves as a reminder of the importance of implementing robust cybersecurity measures to protect sensitive patient data, as required by the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also necessitates that healthcare providers have procedures in place to respond to data breaches in a timely and effective manner. The Australian Cyber Security Centre’s Essential Eight provides a framework for healthcare providers to protect themselves against cyber threats, including the use of encryption and secure storage of sensitive data. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act highlights the need for healthcare providers to prioritize cybersecurity and protect their systems and data from potential threats. In light of this, Australian healthcare providers should review their current cybersecurity practices and ensure they are aligned with the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance to mitigate the risk of similar data breaches occurring in their organisations.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement robust encryption measures to protect sensitive patient data, both in transit and at rest
- Regularly review and update cybersecurity practices to ensure alignment with the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance
- Develop and test incident response plans to ensure timely and effective response to data breaches, as required by the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme
