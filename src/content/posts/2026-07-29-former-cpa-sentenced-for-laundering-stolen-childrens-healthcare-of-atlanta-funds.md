A former certified public accountant in the United States has been sentenced to four years in prison for laundering $5.3 million in stolen funds from Children’s Healthcare of Atlanta, which was the result of a business email compromise attack on one of the healthcare provider's vendors. The attack, which occurred in 2023, involved a hacker compromising the email account of a vendor that supplied furniture and other supplies to Children’s Healthcare of Atlanta. The hacker then impersonated the vendor, requesting a change to the vendor's automatic clearinghouse electronic payment instructions, resulting in the stolen funds being directed to the former CPA's bank account. Australian healthcare providers should be aware of the risks of business email compromise attacks and take steps to protect themselves, including implementing robust email security measures and ensuring that all transactions are verified through multiple channels. Under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, healthcare providers have a responsibility to protect the personal information of their patients, and a breach of this kind could have significant implications under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. The Australian Cyber Security Centre recommends that organisations follow the ASD Essential Eight guidelines to mitigate the risk of cyber attacks, including implementing application whitelisting, patching applications, and restricting administrative privileges. By following these guidelines and staying vigilant, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of falling victim to business email compromise attacks and other types of cybercrime. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act also plays a role in protecting healthcare infrastructure from cyber threats, and healthcare providers should be aware of their obligations under this legislation. In order to prevent similar incidents, it is crucial for healthcare providers to have robust cybersecurity measures in place, including regular security audits and staff training on cybersecurity best practices.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multi-factor authentication for all email accounts and financial transactions to prevent unauthorised access
- Regularly review and update payment instructions and vendor information to prevent fraudulent changes
- Conduct regular security awareness training for staff to educate them on the risks of business email compromise attacks and other types of cybercrime
