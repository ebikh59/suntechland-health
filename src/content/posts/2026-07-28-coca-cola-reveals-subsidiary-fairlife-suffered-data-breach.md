A subsidiary of the Coca-Cola Company, Fairlife, has suffered a data breach following a ransomware attack in July, resulting in the theft of certain data and production outages at its US facilities. The incident, which was reported earlier in July, involved unauthorized access to a portion of the company's systems, with the ransomware group Anubis claiming responsibility and leaking 671GB of data, including HR records, engineering and technical documentation, and production data. The breach highlights the importance of protecting internal business data, as this information can be used by criminals to impersonate executives or vendors, redirect payments, or target employees with phishing messages. Australian healthcare providers should take note of this incident, as it demonstrates the potential consequences of a data breach, including the theft of sensitive information and disruption to business operations. Under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, organisations have a responsibility to protect personal information from unauthorised access, and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires entities to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight provides a framework for organisations to protect themselves against cyber threats, and healthcare providers should consider implementing these measures to prevent similar incidents. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act also highlights the need for organisations to prioritize cybersecurity and protect their systems and data from unauthorized access.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies to prevent ransomware attacks, including patching applications and operating systems, and restricting administrative privileges
- Conduct regular security audits and risk assessments to identify vulnerabilities and weaknesses in systems and data, and implement measures to address these risks
- Develop and implement a comprehensive incident response plan, including procedures for responding to data breaches and notifying affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner
