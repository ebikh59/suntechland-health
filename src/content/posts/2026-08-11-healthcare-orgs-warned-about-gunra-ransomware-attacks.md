Australian healthcare organisations are being warned about the Gunra ransomware-as-a-service operation, which has been targeting government and critical infrastructure entities, including healthcare organisations, globally. The group, first identified in April 2025, has accelerated its attacks in 2026 and is known to target Windows systems, using advanced encryption methods and a Linux variant of its encryptor. Gunra ransomware engages in double extortion attacks, stealing sensitive data before encrypting files, and has been observed gaining access to victims' networks by exploiting known vulnerabilities in internet-facing devices. This poses a significant threat to Australian healthcare providers, who must ensure compliance with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, particularly in relation to the protection of personal and sensitive information. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires healthcare organisations to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. To mitigate the risk of a Gunra ransomware attack, Australian healthcare organisations should prioritise patching for known exploited vulnerabilities, segment their networks to prevent lateral movement, and create immutable backups. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight provides a framework for organisations to protect themselves against cyber threats, and healthcare providers should ensure they are following this guidance to reduce their risk. By taking proactive steps to secure their systems and data, Australian healthcare organisations can minimize the risk of a successful Gunra ransomware attack and protect sensitive patient information. The Secure Online Communication and Information Act also plays a role in protecting healthcare information, and organisations must be aware of their obligations under this legislation. Healthcare organisations must remain vigilant and take immediate action to reduce the risk of an attack, as the consequences of a breach can be severe.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Prioritise patching for known exploited vulnerabilities, especially in VPNs and RDP-exposed infrastructure, as per the ASD Essential Eight
- Segment networks to prevent lateral movement from initially compromised devices to other organisational systems, in line with ACSC guidance
- Create immutable backups and store them in physically separate, segmented locations to ensure data can be recovered without paying the ransom, as recommended by the ACSC
