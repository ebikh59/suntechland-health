A recent data breach at logistics giant Ceva has had significant implications for its European clients, with the company's contract logistics operations being impacted. The breach, which occurred between July 29 and August 1, has resulted in the potential theft of customer data, including names, email and home addresses, phone numbers, and order details. This incident highlights the importance of robust cybersecurity measures for companies that handle sensitive information, particularly those in the logistics sector. For Australian healthcare providers, this breach serves as a reminder of the need to ensure the security of their own supply chain ecosystems, as required by the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also necessitates that healthcare providers notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. In light of this incident, Australian healthcare IT and security teams should be vigilant in monitoring their systems for potential breaches and ensuring compliance with the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance. The SOCI Act also plays a crucial role in regulating the handling of sensitive information, and healthcare providers must be aware of their obligations under this legislation. As the logistics sector is a critical component of the healthcare supply chain, any disruptions or breaches in this sector can have far-reaching consequences for healthcare providers and their patients.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multi-factor authentication to prevent unauthorised access to systems and data
- Regularly update and patch software to prevent exploitation of known vulnerabilities
- Conduct thorough risk assessments of supply chain partners and third-party vendors to identify potential security risks
