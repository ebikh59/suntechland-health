A recent cyberattack on the AnMed health system in the United States has resulted in the temporary closure of 83 facilities, with the organisation warning patients about potential malicious communications. The malware-related attack, which occurred on July 26, 2026, has disrupted the health system's IT systems, including computer systems, phone lines, and internet connectivity. Although AnMed has confirmed that its doctors have access to medical records and the provision of safe care is the highest priority, some patients are facing delays and appointments have been rescheduled. The incident serves as a reminder to Australian healthcare providers of the importance of robust cybersecurity measures, particularly in protecting sensitive patient information. Under the Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles, healthcare providers have a responsibility to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal information. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires organisations to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. In light of this incident, Australian healthcare providers should review their cybersecurity protocols and consider implementing the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies to prevent similar attacks. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's guidance on cybersecurity best practices should also be consulted to ensure the security of patient information. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 highlights the need for healthcare providers to prioritise cybersecurity in their operations. By taking proactive measures to protect against cyber threats, Australian healthcare providers can minimise the risk of similar incidents occurring and ensure the continuity of patient care.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies, including patching applications and operating systems, to prevent malware-related attacks
- Review and update incident response plans to ensure business continuity and minimise disruption to patient services in the event of a cyberattack
- Conduct regular cybersecurity awareness training for staff to educate them on the risks of phishing and other cyber threats, and the importance of verifying the authenticity of electronic communications
