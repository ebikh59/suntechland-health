## What happened

Australian healthcare organisations are adopting artificial intelligence faster than they are putting appropriate governance and security measures in place, potentially exposing themselves to privacy, cyber security and legal risks. That is according to technology disputes lawyer Christy Mellifont, as reported by Pulse+IT.

Ms Mellifont, a partner at law firm Wotton Kearney, made the comments at the AI.Care conference in Melbourne.

*Pulse+IT's full article is behind a subscriber paywall. This brief is limited to its public summary; the analysis below is SunTechLand's, not Ms Mellifont's.*

## Technical and operational significance

The gap between adoption and governance is a recurring theme in 2026. Health services are deploying ambient scribes, AI documentation tools, chatbots and decision support, often through individual departments or clinicians, while governance frameworks and national guidance are still being developed.

The three risk categories identified link closely:

- **Privacy risk** arises when patient data is sent to AI services without adequate assessment of where it goes, how long it is kept and whether it is used for training.
- **Cyber security risk** arises when AI tools add new integrations, accounts and data stores that are not covered by existing security controls.
- **Legal risk** follows from both, through regulatory action, contractual disputes with vendors and claims by affected patients. Unclear accountability for AI-influenced decisions adds to it.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

The comments reinforce a practical message: governance should be in place before AI is scaled, not added after an incident. Contracts with AI vendors are a key control. They define who is responsible for data protection, breach notification, model errors and regulatory compliance.

National guidance from the ACSQHC, TGA reforms on scribes and digital mental health tools, and the government's planned AI safety legislation will all raise expectations. Health services that already have registers, assessments and contractual protections in place will be best placed to meet them.

## What to watch next

- Further legal and regulatory developments on AI in healthcare, including the outcome of the ACSQHC consultation.
- The first Australian disputes or regulatory actions involving AI tools in healthcare.
- Privacy Act reforms affecting AI and automated decision-making.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Maintain a register of AI tools in use across the organisation, including those adopted by individual departments or clinicians.
- Require privacy impact and security assessments before AI tools process patient information.
- Review AI vendor contracts for data use, retention, training rights, breach notification, liability and regulatory compliance.
- Define accountability for decisions informed by AI, and document clinician review requirements.
- Involve legal, privacy and security teams in AI procurement and governance, not only clinical and digital teams.
