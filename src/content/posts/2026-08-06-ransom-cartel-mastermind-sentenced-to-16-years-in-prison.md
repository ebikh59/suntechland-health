A key figure behind the Ransom Cartel ransomware group has been sentenced to 16 years in prison for his role in orchestrating ransomware attacks on at least 18 companies worldwide, resulting in over $6.7 million in losses. The mastermind, Maksim Silnikau, was the creator and administrator of the Ransom Cartel ransomware-as-a-service operation, which recruited other cybercriminals to conduct attacks globally. This sentence serves as a warning to cybercriminals that their actions will not go unpunished, and it highlights the importance of robust cybersecurity measures for Australian healthcare providers. Under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, healthcare providers have a responsibility to protect sensitive patient information from cyber threats. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires healthcare providers to notify the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner and affected individuals in the event of a data breach. Australian healthcare providers should take note of the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance to mitigate the risk of ransomware attacks, which can have devastating consequences for both the organisation and its patients. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act also plays a crucial role in protecting Australia's critical infrastructure, including healthcare, from cyber threats. By prioritising cybersecurity and implementing robust measures, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of falling victim to ransomware attacks and protect sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multi-factor authentication to prevent unauthorised access to systems and data
- Regularly update and patch software to prevent exploitation of known vulnerabilities
- Use application whitelisting to restrict the execution of unapproved software and scripts
