A recent analysis of cyber incidents that required remediation has found that phishing attacks were the dominant method of initial entry, accounting for just over half of incidents investigated during the last quarter. This represents a significant rise compared to the previous quarter, where phishing attacks represented the initial entry point for a third of incidents. The rise in phishing coincides with a period where attackers have been experimenting with new tools and techniques to socially engineer victims and evade cybersecurity protections. For instance, a QR code phishing campaign targeted organisations to harvest login credentials and further propagate attacks by automatically targeting victims' contacts. This campaign used auto-generated victim-tailored PDF documents containing QR codes that directed victims to adversary-controlled Microsoft 365 credential harvesting pages. Australian healthcare providers must be vigilant, as these types of attacks can have significant implications under the Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles, particularly in relation to the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. The use of phishing-as-a-service kits with expanded capabilities is also a concern, as these kits provide cybercriminals with a suite of tools to make attacks more effective. Healthcare providers should review their cybersecurity measures, including multi-factor authentication and email security gateways, to prevent such attacks. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight provides a framework for organisations to protect themselves against cyber threats, and healthcare providers should ensure they are implementing these measures to prevent phishing attacks. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act highlights the importance of protecting critical infrastructure, including healthcare services, from cyber threats. By prioritising cybersecurity and implementing robust measures, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of phishing attacks and protect sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication on all accounts, particularly those with access to sensitive patient information
- Block or flag emails containing QR codes within PDF attachments to prevent phishing attacks
- Monitor for suspicious inbox rule creation and anomalous file staging as indicators of post-compromise activity, and ensure regular security updates and patches are applied to all systems
