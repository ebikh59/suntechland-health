## What happened

Health systems risk automating existing problems rather than improving care if artificial intelligence is introduced without the governance, workforce capability and organisational systems needed to use it effectively. That is the warning from Professor Jeffrey Braithwaite, founding director of the Australian Institute of Health Innovation at Macquarie University, as reported by Pulse+IT.

Professor Braithwaite, a leading Australian patient safety researcher, has examined the role of AI in healthcare. The headline of Pulse+IT's report sums up his message: AI prediction is not prevention.

*Pulse+IT's full article is behind a subscriber paywall. This brief is limited to its public summary, and the analysis below is SunTechLand's, not Professor Braithwaite's.*

## Technical and operational significance

The distinction between prediction and prevention is central to clinical AI. A model that accurately predicts which patients will deteriorate, be readmitted or develop a complication only improves outcomes if the organisation can act on that prediction. That requires the right staff, time and processes to intervene, and clear responsibility for doing so.

Without those, AI can add alerts to already stretched teams, increase alert fatigue, and create a record of risks that were flagged but not acted on. Errors and biases in existing processes can also be reproduced and scaled.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

For Australian health services, the message supports the direction of national AI governance guidance: organisational readiness, accountability and monitoring matter as much as model accuracy. Before deploying a predictive tool, services should confirm who will act on its outputs and how.

This also applies to security tools. AI-driven threat detection only reduces risk if security teams have the capacity and processes to respond to what it finds.

## What to watch next

- Publication of Professor Braithwaite's detailed findings.
- National guidance on evaluating whether AI tools improve outcomes, not just predictions.
- Evidence from Australian deployments of predictive AI in clinical settings.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Before deploying predictive AI, define who will act on each type of alert, within what timeframe, and with what resources.
- Evaluate AI tools on whether they change outcomes, not only on predictive accuracy.
- Monitor alert volumes and response rates to detect alert fatigue and unaddressed risks.
- Assess whether existing processes contain biases or weaknesses that AI could amplify, and address them first.
- Apply the same readiness test to AI-driven security tools: confirm response capacity before adding new detection capability.
