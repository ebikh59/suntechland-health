A recent data breach at a California-based non-profit organisation has highlighted the risks associated with employee practices and the potential for long-term exposure of sensitive data. Although the organisation is not subject to the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Rules, the incident serves as a reminder to Australian healthcare providers of the importance of protecting personal data in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The breach occurred when an employee forwarded internal files containing personal data to an external email account over a period of nine years, resulting in a significant loss of control over the data and potential vulnerability to unauthorised access. Fortunately, the investigation found no evidence of unauthorised data access or misuse, but the organisation has still taken steps to notify affected individuals and offer them complimentary credit monitoring and identity theft protection services. This incident underscores the need for Australian healthcare providers to implement robust security measures, including regular security awareness training and technical controls to prevent similar incidents. The Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB) scheme and the Security of Critical Infrastructure (SOCI) Act also emphasise the importance of prompt notification and effective incident response in the event of a data breach. By prioritising data protection and security, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of similar incidents and maintain the trust of their patients and stakeholders. The Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC) guidance and the Essential Eight mitigation strategies provide a framework for organisations to improve their cybersecurity posture and protect against common threats. By following these guidelines and staying vigilant, Australian healthcare providers can minimise the risk of data breaches and ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of sensitive patient data.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement regular security awareness training for employees to emphasize the risks associated with emailing or copying sensitive data to external accounts
- Configure technical security measures to block or flag anomalous behaviour when emailing internal data to external email accounts
- Review and update incident response plans to ensure prompt notification and effective response in the event of a data breach, in accordance with the Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB) scheme and ACSC guidance
