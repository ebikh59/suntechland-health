A US-based telehealth company, Him & Hers, is facing a lawsuit from the Federal Trade Commission and the states of Utah and California over its business and data sharing practices. The company, which provides prescription and over-the-counter medications, allegedly discloses sensitive consumer data to third-party advertising platforms without consent. This raises significant concerns for Australian healthcare providers, who must comply with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, which mandate the protection of personal information. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires entities to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. The allegations against Him & Hers highlight the importance of transparency in data handling practices and the need for healthcare providers to clearly disclose how consumer information is used and shared. Australian healthcare providers must ensure that their data sharing practices are compliant with local regulations and that consumers are fully informed about how their personal information is being used. The use of tracking technologies and advertising tools by Him & Hers also underscores the need for healthcare providers to be mindful of the security risks associated with these technologies and to implement appropriate mitigations, such as those outlined in the ASD Essential Eight. By prioritising data protection and security, Australian healthcare providers can build trust with their patients and maintain the confidentiality of sensitive medical information. The lawsuit against Him & Hers serves as a reminder of the importance of robust data protection practices in the healthcare sector. Australian healthcare providers should review their own data handling practices to ensure they are compliant with relevant regulations and guidelines, including those provided by the ACSC. This includes being transparent about data sharing practices, obtaining informed consent from consumers, and implementing robust security measures to protect sensitive information. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act also highlights the need for healthcare providers to prioritise cybersecurity and protect against potential threats. By taking a proactive approach to data protection and security, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of data breaches and maintain the trust of their patients.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Review and update data sharing practices to ensure transparency and compliance with the Australian Privacy Principles
- Implement robust security measures, such as those outlined in the ASD Essential Eight, to protect sensitive consumer information
- Regularly review and update cybersecurity controls, including those related to tracking technologies and advertising tools, to ensure alignment with ACSC guidance and the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act
