A series of data breaches have been announced by five small healthcare organisations in the United States, highlighting the ongoing risks to patient data. The breaches, which occurred at Family Medical Associates of Raleigh, Arkansas Oral & Maxillofacial Surgeons, Alpine Agency of the Midlands, Princeton Family Eye Care, and James C. Standring, DDS, resulted in the exposure of sensitive patient information, including names, demographic information, contact information, medical and treatment information, and health insurance information. For Australian healthcare providers, these breaches serve as a reminder of the importance of robust cybersecurity measures to protect patient data, as required under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires healthcare organisations to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight provides a framework for healthcare organisations to protect themselves against cyber threats, and following this guidance is crucial to preventing similar breaches in Australia. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act also imposes obligations on healthcare organisations to protect their systems and data from cyber threats. Australian healthcare organisations must remain vigilant and take proactive steps to protect patient data, including implementing robust cybersecurity measures and regularly reviewing their systems for vulnerabilities. By doing so, they can reduce the risk of a data breach and ensure compliance with relevant laws and regulations. The breaches announced by the five small healthcare organisations in the United States demonstrate that even small organisations can be targeted by cyber attackers, and Australian healthcare providers must be prepared to respond to similar threats.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies to prevent cyber threats
- Regularly review and update cybersecurity systems and procedures to ensure compliance with the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme and the Australian Privacy Principles
- Provide training to staff on cybersecurity best practices and the importance of protecting patient data
