A recent spate of settlements by five healthcare providers in class action lawsuits over the use of website tracking and analytics tools has significant implications for Australian healthcare organisations. The lawsuits, which originated in the United States, centre on the use of tracking tools such as pixels, which can potentially disclose sensitive patient data to third parties. While these tools may not necessarily result in a compensable injury, as seen in the dismissal of a proposed class action lawsuit against CRH Healthcare, they can still pose a risk to patient privacy. Australian healthcare providers must be aware of their obligations under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, particularly in relation to the handling of personal and sensitive information. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires organisations to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. In light of these settlements, Australian healthcare providers should review their use of tracking and analytics tools to ensure they are complying with Australian privacy laws and taking steps to mitigate potential risks. This includes implementing robust security measures, such as those outlined in the ASD Essential Eight, and following ACSC guidance on protecting sensitive information. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure (SOCI) Act highlights the need for organisations to prioritise the security and integrity of their systems and data. By taking proactive steps to protect patient data, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of similar class action lawsuits and maintain the trust of their patients.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Conduct a review of all website tracking and analytics tools to ensure they are necessary and proportionate to the intended purpose
- Implement additional security controls, such as data loss prevention tools, to mitigate the risk of sensitive data disclosure
- Provide training to staff on the importance of handling personal and sensitive information in accordance with Australian privacy laws and the Australian Privacy Principles
