## What happened

St Vincent's has called on the Australian Government to prioritise health and aged care in its AI standards and infrastructure policies. It warned that Australia risks becoming dependent on overseas technology providers without greater investment in local capability, Pulse+IT reported.

The call was made in a submission to the government's consultation on AI standards and frontier AI infrastructure. According to IT Brief, St Vincent's asked that health and aged care be treated as priority sectors in Australia's first AI standards. It also warned about losing control of healthcare AI development.

The submission framed a central choice between building local systems and buying them from foreign suppliers. It argued that a domestic approach would support local expertise and make better use of Australian population health data. It also said AI investment in healthcare should not be left entirely to commercial incentives, and called for public-interest obligations on companies developing AI in Australia, with contributions directed to health and aged care research.

St Vincent's operates 12 public and private hospitals and 25 aged care facilities. It recently established a national AI Clinical Council to guide AI adoption across its services.

## Technical and operational significance

Dependence on overseas AI providers has several practical dimensions for health organisations:

- **Data location and jurisdiction.** Patient data processed by offshore AI services may be subject to foreign laws and access regimes.
- **Supply continuity.** Changes in a foreign provider's pricing, terms, availability or regulatory position can disrupt services that health organisations rely on.
- **Transparency and influence.** Australian health services have limited ability to shape or audit models developed elsewhere.
- **Alignment with local context.** Models trained mainly on overseas populations may perform less well for Australian patients, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people.

Data sovereignty and residency are already important in health procurement, and many vendors now offer onshore hosting. Onshore hosting of a foreign model, however, does not resolve every concern about control, transparency and continuity.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

The submission highlights a strategic question for health services as much as for government. Every procurement decision about AI tools is, in part, a decision about where data goes, who controls the models, and how resilient services will be if a provider changes course.

In the near term, health services can reduce risk through procurement requirements: data residency, contractual limits on secondary use of patient data, exit and portability provisions, transparency about underlying models, and evidence of local validation.

## What to watch next

- The government's response to submissions on AI standards and frontier AI infrastructure.
- Policy measures on public-interest obligations for AI developers.
- Investment in domestic AI capability for health and aged care.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Require data residency and clear limits on the secondary use of patient data in contracts for AI services.
- Ask vendors to disclose which underlying AI models they use, where they are hosted, and which jurisdictions' laws apply.
- Include exit, portability and continuity provisions in AI contracts in case a provider changes terms or withdraws a service.
- Ask for evidence that AI tools have been validated on Australian patient populations, including diverse and First Nations groups.
- Engage with national AI standards consultations to represent health and aged care requirements.
