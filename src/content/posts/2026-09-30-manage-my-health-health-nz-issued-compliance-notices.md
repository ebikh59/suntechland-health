## What happened

New Zealand Privacy Commissioner Michael Webster has issued compliance notices to patient portal provider Manage My Health and to Health New Zealand. The notices concern security failings identified after the December 2025 cyber attack on Manage My Health. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner lists the notices as issued on 23 September 2026.

The notices relate to failures to comply with rule 5 of the Health Information Privacy Code, which requires agencies holding health information to have reasonable security safeguards. They follow the Commissioner's Phase 1 inquiry report, published in May 2026, which found that both organisations had breached rule 5. The OPC said 91% of affected patients were based in Northland. Reporting puts the number of affected patients at about 99,000, revised down from an initial estimate of 126,000.

According to The Lawyer, the Manage My Health notice addresses rule 5(1)(a) and must be completed by 31 August 2027. The Health NZ notice addresses rule 5(1)(b), which covers protecting health information before it is given to a service provider, with a deadline of 29 January 2027. IAPP reported that the Health NZ notice requires privacy assessments during procurement and a privacy impact assessment covering information flows at the system design stage.

Commissioner Webster has described compliance notices as the strongest tool available to him for serious privacy breaches.

## Technical and operational significance

The notices split responsibility between the platform operator and the health agency that supplied it with data. That is the significant part.

Manage My Health is responsible for the security of the portal itself. Health NZ, however, is being held accountable for how it decided to share patient information with a third-party provider, including whether it assessed the provider's security and the privacy risks of the data flows before sending data.

This reflects a principle increasingly emphasised by privacy regulators: outsourcing data handling does not outsource accountability. An organisation that sends patient information to a vendor must take reasonable steps to make sure the vendor can protect it, and must design the arrangement with privacy in mind from the start.

Reporting by 1News on the Phase 1 inquiry indicated Manage My Health had been warned about security issues before the breach. That makes the "reasonable safeguards" question sharper.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

Australian health services share patient data with many third parties: patient portals, booking platforms, telehealth providers, pathology and imaging networks, and software vendors. Under the Privacy Act, Australian Privacy Principle 11 requires reasonable steps to protect personal information. The OAIC has made clear that organisations remain responsible for information they disclose to contractors.

The New Zealand notices give a concrete model of what regulators may expect. That includes privacy and security assessment at procurement, privacy impact assessment when designing information flows, and continuing oversight of vendor security. Australian health services that cannot show these steps for their patient-facing platforms would be exposed if a vendor were breached.

## What to watch next

- Progress reports on both organisations' compliance with the notices, and any changes to deadlines.
- The Commissioner's final Phase 2 report and its recommendations.
- Whether Australian regulators adopt similar expectations for health agencies that share data with portal providers.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Complete a privacy impact assessment before sharing patient data with any new platform or vendor, covering what data flows where and why.
- Include security and privacy due diligence in procurement for patient portals and data-sharing services, with evidence of controls rather than self-assessment alone.
- Share only the minimum patient information each vendor needs, and review those data flows periodically.
- Contract for regular security assurance, prompt breach notification and remediation of identified vulnerabilities by vendors.
- Act on security warnings about vendor platforms quickly, and document the decisions taken.
