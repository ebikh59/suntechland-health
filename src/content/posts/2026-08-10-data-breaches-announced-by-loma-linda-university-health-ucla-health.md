A recent data security incident at Loma Linda University Health involved the inadvertent upload of patient information to an external AI platform, compromising medical record numbers, dates of birth, and limited clinical information related to orthopedic care. This incident highlights the risks associated with the use of external technologies, such as AI platforms, in healthcare settings. Similarly, UCLA Health has notified patients of an improper disclosure of their personal and protected health information to an outside healthcare provider, with the breach occurring between December 27, 2024, and April 21, 2026. The disclosed information included names, dates of birth, health insurance information, and clinical information, with some individuals' Social Security numbers also being exposed. These incidents serve as a reminder to Australian healthcare providers of the importance of ensuring the privacy and security of patient information, as required by the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, entities must notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of an eligible data breach. The ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance provide a framework for healthcare organisations to protect themselves against cyber threats, including the use of external technologies. Australian healthcare providers must review their policies, procedures, and workforce training on the use of external technologies to prevent similar breaches from occurring. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 also plays a role in protecting Australia's critical infrastructure, including healthcare, from cyber threats. By prioritising cybersecurity and complying with relevant regulations, Australian healthcare providers can mitigate the risk of data breaches and protect sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies to prevent cyber threats, including the use of external technologies such as AI platforms
- Regularly review and update policies, procedures, and workforce training on the use of external technologies to ensure compliance with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles
- Conduct regular security audits and risk assessments to identify vulnerabilities and implement additional measures to prevent data breaches, as outlined in ACSC guidance
