A recent analysis has revealed that the average cost of a data breach has risen to almost $5 million, with the global average breach cost climbing 12% during the last year. This significant increase in costs is largely attributed to lost business costs, either due to immediate loss of sales or long-term loss of customer trust. Cybercriminals are well aware of this risk and are adapting their tactics, particularly with ransomware and extortion attacks, to apply pressure on victims by threatening to damage their brand reputation. The healthcare sector has been hit the hardest, with the highest average breach cost for the 13th consecutive year, at $6.6 million. This is largely due to the value placed on patient personally identifiable information, which can be used for identity theft, insurance fraud, and other financial crimes. The rise of AI-powered attacks has also had a significant impact on the attack landscape, with over one in four organisations experiencing AI-driven attacks, representing a 56% increase from the previous year. Australian healthcare providers must take heed of these findings and ensure they are taking adequate measures to protect sensitive patient information, in line with the Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles, to avoid the costly consequences of a data breach. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires organisations to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a breach, making it essential for healthcare providers to have robust incident response plans in place. Furthermore, implementing the ASD Essential Eight and following ACSC guidance can help mitigate the risk of cyber-attacks and reduce the financial impact of a breach. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act also plays a crucial role in protecting healthcare infrastructure from cyber threats. As the use of AI-powered attacks continues to rise, it is essential for Australian healthcare providers to stay vigilant and adapt their security measures to combat these emerging threats.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies to prevent cyber-attacks and reduce the risk of a data breach
- Develop a robust incident response plan to quickly respond to and contain a breach, in line with the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme and ACSC guidance
- Conduct regular security audits and risk assessments to identify vulnerabilities and Vectors for AI-powered attacks, and update security measures accordingly
