## What happened

The Australian Digital Health Agency (ADHA) is strengthening clinical governance around artificial intelligence and virtual care as growing numbers of patients use AI tools to interpret medical results and make decisions about their health, Pulse+IT reported.

The update was given at the Royal Australian College of General Practitioners' GP26 conference in Adelaide by ADHA chief clinical advisers, including Dr Amandeep Hansra.

Dr Hansra chairs the National Clinical Governance Committee for Digital Health, which ADHA announced in March 2026. The committee's remit covers information sharing through My Health Record, patient safety and quality in virtual care and telehealth, and the use of AI in healthcare.

*Pulse+IT's full article is behind a subscriber paywall. This brief is limited to its public summary and publicly available information about the committee; the analysis below is SunTechLand's.*

## Technical and operational significance

Patients using general-purpose AI tools to interpret their own results is a shift that health services do not control. Patients may upload pathology reports, imaging results or discharge summaries into consumer chatbots, sometimes downloaded from My Health Record or patient portals.

That raises several issues:

- **Accuracy and safety.** Consumer AI tools may misinterpret results or give advice that conflicts with the treating clinician.
- **Privacy.** Health information uploaded to consumer AI services leaves the protections of the health system and may be retained or used by those services.
- **Clinical relationships.** GPs increasingly need to discuss AI-generated interpretations with patients.

Health services cannot prevent patients from using these tools. They can, however, provide guidance, design portals and results communication to reduce confusion, and make sure clinicians are prepared to discuss AI use.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

General practices and other providers should expect patients to arrive with AI-generated interpretations of their results. Clear patient information about the risks of uploading health information to consumer AI services, and about when to seek clinical advice, would help.

For organisations running patient portals, the trend increases the importance of security and privacy controls on downloads and exports, and of clear explanations alongside results.

## What to watch next

- Guidance from the National Clinical Governance Committee for Digital Health on AI and virtual care.
- Patient-facing advice from ADHA on using AI tools with health records.
- Evidence on how patients' use of AI affects safety and care.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Provide patients with plain-language guidance on the privacy and safety risks of uploading health records into consumer AI tools.
- Prepare clinicians to discuss patient-generated AI interpretations of results and to correct errors.
- Present results in portals with clear explanations and guidance on when to seek clinical advice.
- Review security controls on patient portal downloads and record exports.
- Follow national clinical governance guidance on AI and virtual care as it is released, and align local policies with it.
