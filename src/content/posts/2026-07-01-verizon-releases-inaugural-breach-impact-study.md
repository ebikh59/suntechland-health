## What happened

Verizon Business has published its inaugural Breach Impact Study, produced with CyberAcuView by the team behind its annual Data Breach Investigations Report. The study analyses about 70,000 US cyber insurance claims from January 2019 to October 2025, including 38,000 where policies paid out, and reports medians rather than averages.

The headline finding is that "data breach costs almost doubled between 2019 and 2025". The median impact rose 80%, from about US$60,000 in 2019 to US$110,000 in 2025, outpacing inflation of about 23% over the period. More than half of paid claims exceeded US$83,000, 10% had an impact of US$920,000 or more, and the top 2.5% exceeded US$5 million.

Business interruption was the largest driver of losses, followed by losses to threat actors and response and recovery costs. Software supply chain and third-party incidents made up only about 2% of claims, but business interruption accounted for 50% of their losses, and extreme cases exceeded US$100 million. Median impact was about US$38,000 for small and medium businesses, US$96,000 for mid-market firms and US$238,000 for large enterprises.

## Technical and operational significance

The healthcare figures stand out. The study includes more than 8,640 healthcare claims, 5,100 with recorded losses, and healthcare accounted for 23% of total losses across the dataset. Its median liability loss was 57% higher than the overall figure.

Ransomware was the most common type of healthcare claim (39%) and accounted for 60% of healthcare costs, with a median of US$77,051. Business email compromise appeared in 22% of healthcare cases and 10% of cost, with a median of US$94,924.

Two practical conclusions follow:

- **Downtime is the main cost.** Because business interruption dominates losses, investment in resilience, including segmentation, tested backups and recovery plans, directly reduces financial impact.
- **Third-party incidents are rare but severe.** Supply chain incidents are uncommon, but when they happen they can be catastrophic, which supports focusing third-party risk effort on the small number of suppliers whose failure would stop operations.

The study reflects US insured losses. Uninsured organisations, costs outside policy coverage and impacts on patients are not captured.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

US dollar figures do not translate directly to Australia, but the pattern is relevant. Healthcare organisations everywhere face the combination of ransomware-driven downtime and high liability exposure. Australian health services also carry obligations under the Privacy Act and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, and some hospitals have critical infrastructure obligations under the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act.

The prominence of business email compromise in healthcare claims is a reminder that invoice and payment-redirection fraud deserves attention alongside ransomware. Health services pay many suppliers and handle patient refunds, both targets for BEC.

For boards and executives, the study offers evidence that ransomware resilience and recovery capability are the controls most likely to limit financial loss.

## What to watch next

- Future editions of the study, and whether healthcare's share of losses changes.
- Similar claims-based analysis for Australian insurers and organisations.
- Cyber insurers' underwriting requirements for healthcare, which tend to follow loss experience.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Prioritise ransomware resilience: segment clinical and corporate networks, maintain offline or immutable backups, and test restoration of critical clinical systems.
- Develop and exercise business continuity plans that keep clinical services running during prolonged IT outages.
- Strengthen controls against business email compromise, including supplier bank detail verification by phone and phishing-resistant MFA on email.
- Identify the small number of suppliers whose compromise would halt operations, and focus third-party assurance and contingency planning on them.
- Review cyber insurance coverage against realistic business interruption scenarios for your organisation.
