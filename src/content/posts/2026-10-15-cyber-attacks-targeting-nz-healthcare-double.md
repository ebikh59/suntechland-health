## What happened

Cyber incidents of potential national significance targeting New Zealand's healthcare and social assistance sector doubled in 2025-26, according to the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), as reported by Pulse+IT. The sector accounted for 8% of all such incidents, up from 4% the previous year, with approximately 30 potentially nationally significant incidents affecting it.

The figures come from the NCSC's annual Cyber Threat Report, released on 24 September 2026. Across all sectors, the NCSC handled 369 incidents of potential national significance in the 2025-26 financial year. Of these, 162 were linked to criminal or financially motivated actors, an 18% increase on the previous year.

The NCSC also recorded four incidents classified as C2, or "Highly Significant", during the year. NCSC head Catriona Robinson said that was as many in one year as had been recorded over the previous ten years combined.

The period covered includes the December 2025 cyber attack on the Manage My Health patient portal, which exposed the records of tens of thousands of patients, mostly in Northland.

## Technical and operational significance

A doubling of the healthcare share in a single year is a significant shift. It reflects a broader trend: financially motivated groups target health organisations because they hold valuable data and cannot tolerate long outages, which increases the pressure to pay.

The rise in "Highly Significant" incidents suggests that the most damaging attacks are becoming more frequent, not just more numerous. For health organisations, a highly significant incident typically means prolonged disruption to clinical systems, large-scale data exposure, or both.

New Zealand's health sector includes Health NZ, primary care practices, patient portals and many private providers and suppliers, which gives it a broad attack surface. Third-party platforms such as portals and practice management systems have been a recurring weak point.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

Australia and New Zealand share many digital health vendors, portal providers and practice management systems, and attackers often operate across both markets. A rising trend in New Zealand healthcare is a strong signal for Australian providers, especially those using the same suppliers.

Australian health services should make sure they are receiving threat intelligence through the ACSC Partnership Program and sector groups. They should also treat shared trans-Tasman vendors as part of their risk assessment. The ACSC's own annual cyber threat reporting has also consistently identified healthcare as one of the most frequently affected sectors.

## What to watch next

- The breakdown of incident types affecting NZ healthcare, including ransomware and third-party compromises.
- Further government investment or regulation in New Zealand's health cyber security.
- Overlap with Australian incidents involving shared vendors.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Subscribe to ACSC and NCSC NZ advisories and join sector threat-sharing groups to receive early warnings, including about trans-Tasman vendors.
- Identify suppliers used in both Australia and New Zealand, and include incidents affecting them in your risk monitoring.
- Assess third-party portals and platforms that hold patient data, and require evidence of security controls and breach notification commitments.
- Exercise incident response for a "highly significant" scenario, including prolonged clinical system outage and large-scale data exposure.
- Apply the ASD Essential Eight baseline across clinical and corporate systems, prioritising MFA, patching and backups.
