## What happened

Consultation on draft national guidance for the adoption and governance of artificial intelligence across Australian health services closes on Monday 30 November 2026. The Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC) is seeking feedback on its draft AI Guidance for Health Services.

According to Pulse+IT, the draft includes recommendations covering organisational accountability, procurement and ongoing monitoring of AI systems. The Commission says the guidance sets out key considerations for health services in the adoption, use and governance of AI. It will also support implementation of the third edition of the National Safety and Quality Health Service (NSQHS) Standards.

The consultation opened on 5 November. The Commission is seeking feedback from health service leaders, including chief executives, executive directors and senior managers, as well as health professionals and consumers. Responses can be made through an online survey or by written submission. Final guidance is expected to be published in 2027.

The draft adds to the Commission's existing AI material, including its AI Clinical Use Guide for clinicians.

## Technical and operational significance

Linking AI guidance to the NSQHS Standards matters because those standards underpin accreditation for Australian hospitals and day procedure services. Guidance that supports their implementation is likely to shape what assessors look for when reviewing how a health service governs AI.

The three areas highlighted map closely onto security and privacy practice:

- **Organisational accountability.** This means knowing who owns each AI system, who approved it and who responds when it fails. The same clarity is needed for incident response and breach management.
- **Procurement.** Procurement is where health services can require evidence of safety testing, security controls, data handling practices and incident notification from vendors.
- **Ongoing monitoring.** AI systems change as models are updated and as use expands. Monitoring for performance, drift and unexpected behaviour overlaps with security monitoring for misuse or compromise.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

Health services that already govern AI through clinical governance committees should check their arrangements against the draft. Those that do not yet have a formal approach should expect AI governance to become part of accreditation expectations.

Security, privacy and digital health teams should be involved in responding to the consultation and in implementing the final guidance, not only clinical governance teams. Many of the risks the guidance addresses, including vendor data handling, unauthorised access and model behaviour, are security and privacy issues as much as clinical ones.

## What to watch next

- The final guidance in 2027 and how it is reflected in NSQHS accreditation.
- Alignment between the Commission's guidance, TGA regulation of AI medical devices and the government's planned AI safety legislation.
- Consumer feedback on transparency and consent for AI use in care.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Review the draft guidance and, if possible, submit feedback before 30 November, including from security and privacy teams.
- Establish or confirm a register of AI systems in use, with a named accountable owner for each.
- Build AI-specific requirements into procurement, covering safety evidence, data handling, security controls and incident notification.
- Set up ongoing monitoring for AI systems that covers clinical performance, model changes and unexpected or unauthorised behaviour.
- Prepare to show evidence of AI governance during NSQHS accreditation once the final guidance is published.
