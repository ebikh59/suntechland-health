## What happened

Pulse+IT has published a Q&A with Paul Chua, Asia Pacific operations director at Health-ISAC, the global healthcare information sharing and analysis centre. The interview asks how effectively the health sector is managing cyber security risks across its digital supply chains, as organisations rely increasingly on third parties for clinical software, cloud infrastructure and data exchange.

Mr Chua joined Health-ISAC in January 2026 after serving as Regional Cybersecurity Officer at medical technology company Becton Dickinson.

The interview is framed around the 2024 MediSecure data breach, the clearest Australian example of supply chain risk in healthcare. MediSecure, an electronic prescription delivery provider, detected ransomware on a database server in April 2024. Personal and health information of about 12.9 million Australians, people who had used the service between March 2019 and November 2023, was stolen. The OAIC said it was the largest number of people ever notified under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. MediSecure went into voluntary administration, and its administrators said they could not identify exactly who was affected.

## Technical and operational significance

MediSecure illustrates several features of healthcare supply chain risk:

- **Concentration.** A single, relatively small supplier held data on about half of Australia's population because it sat at a critical point in the prescribing system.
- **Legacy data.** Much of the stolen data related to a service that had been largely replaced, showing how data held for past functions remains at risk.
- **Weak recovery capacity.** When the company failed financially, the people affected were left without clear notification or support.
- **Indirect relationships.** Many patients had never heard of MediSecure. Their data reached it through the systems their doctors and pharmacists used.

These patterns are common across healthcare, where clinical software vendors, integration engines, cloud platforms, messaging services and outsourced IT providers all handle patient data, often in ways that the health service and its patients cannot easily see.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

Third-party risk management in Australian healthcare often relies on questionnaires at procurement, followed by limited ongoing oversight. MediSecure suggests a more risk-based approach is needed. That means identifying which suppliers hold the most data or provide the most critical services, and focusing assurance, contractual obligations and contingency planning on them.

Information sharing is part of the answer. Organisations such as Health-ISAC and the ACSC Partnership Program help health services learn quickly about compromises affecting shared suppliers, sometimes before suppliers disclose them.

## What to watch next

- Further government and regulator action on supply chain security in digital health, including e-prescribing and messaging infrastructure.
- Health-ISAC's growth and activity in the Asia-Pacific region.
- Supplier incidents affecting multiple Australian health services.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Identify the suppliers that hold the most patient data or provide the most critical clinical services, and prioritise them for security assurance.
- Require suppliers to delete or return data that is no longer needed for the service, including data from legacy or replaced systems.
- Include breach notification, cooperation and support for affected individuals in supplier contracts, with consideration of supplier insolvency.
- Join Health-ISAC or the ACSC Partnership Program to receive early warnings about compromises affecting shared suppliers.
- Develop contingency plans for the loss of critical suppliers, such as e-prescribing, messaging and practice management systems.
