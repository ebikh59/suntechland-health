A recent data breach at Unlimited Technology Systems, a US-based provider of revenue cycle management services, has affected a staggering 3.8 million patients, making it the largest healthcare data breach of the year to date. The breach, which was first identified in October 2025, involved the protected health information of individuals, including names, addresses, email addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, health insurance information, patient balance information, Social Security numbers, medical information, and scanned documents such as driver's licenses. Australian healthcare providers should take note of this incident, as it highlights the importance of ensuring the security of patient data, particularly when working with business associates. Under the Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles, healthcare providers have a responsibility to protect sensitive patient information, and breaches of this nature can have serious consequences. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires healthcare providers to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach, and the Australian Cyber Security Centre provides guidance on how to prevent and respond to cyber threats. The ASD Essential Eight provides a framework for healthcare providers to protect themselves against cyber threats, and ACSC guidance provides additional advice on how to secure sensitive information. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act also plays a role in protecting healthcare infrastructure from cyber threats. This breach serves as a reminder for Australian healthcare providers to review their own cybersecurity measures and ensure they are taking all necessary steps to protect patient data. By prioritising cybersecurity and working with trusted business associates, healthcare providers can reduce the risk of a data breach and protect sensitive patient information. The incident also underscores the importance of having robust incident response plans in place, as well as providing training and awareness programs for staff to identify and respond to potential cyber threats. Ultimately, the security of patient data is a top priority for healthcare providers, and incidents like this serve as a reminder of the need for ongoing vigilance and investment in cybersecurity measures.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multi-factor authentication to prevent unauthorised access to systems and data
- Regularly update and patch software and systems to prevent exploitation of known vulnerabilities
- Conduct regular security audits and risk assessments to identify and address potential security weaknesses
