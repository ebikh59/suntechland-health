A recent data breach settlement in the United States has significant implications for Australian healthcare providers, highlighting the importance of robust cybersecurity measures to protect sensitive patient data. The settlement, which received preliminary approval, resolves class action data breach complaints against Highland Health Systems and Albany Gastroenterology Consultants. The data breach, which occurred in July 2023, resulted in the theft of sensitive patient data, including names, contact information, and medical information, affecting over 83,000 individuals. The lawsuit alleged that the data breach was the result of negligence and could have been prevented with appropriate cybersecurity measures. Australian healthcare providers must take note of this incident, as it underscores the need to implement and maintain effective security controls to prevent similar breaches, in line with the Australian Privacy Principles and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme under the Privacy Act 1988. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight mitigation strategies and guidance from the ACSC can help organisations protect against cyber threats. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 requires certain healthcare providers to implement robust cybersecurity measures to protect critical infrastructure. In the event of a data breach, Australian healthcare providers must notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, as required by the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. The settlement highlights the potential financial and reputational consequences of a data breach, with Highland Health Systems agreeing to establish a $650,000 settlement fund to cover costs and provide benefits to affected individuals. Australian healthcare providers must prioritise cybersecurity and take proactive steps to prevent data breaches, including implementing robust security controls, conducting regular risk assessments, and providing training to staff on cybersecurity best practices. By doing so, they can reduce the risk of a data breach and protect sensitive patient data. The incident also serves as a reminder for Australian healthcare providers to review their cybersecurity posture and ensure compliance with relevant regulations and guidelines, including the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance. Ultimately, the settlement serves as a reminder of the importance of prioritising cybersecurity in the healthcare sector, and Australian healthcare providers must take heed of this warning to protect sensitive patient data and maintain trust in the healthcare system.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement and regularly update cybersecurity controls, such as multi-factor authentication and patching, to prevent cyber threats
- Conduct regular risk assessments to identify vulnerabilities and VDPs, and address them promptly
- Provide training to staff on cybersecurity best practices, including phishing and password management, to reduce the risk of a data breach
