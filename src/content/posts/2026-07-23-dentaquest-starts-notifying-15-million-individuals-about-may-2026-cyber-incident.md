A major cybersecurity incident has occurred at DentaQuest, a dental benefits administrator, with at least 15 million individuals affected, although this number could potentially rise to over 23.4 million as the data review continues. The incident, which involved unauthorized access to DentaQuest's computer network between May 17 and May 20, 2026, has led to the exposure of sensitive information including names, addresses, Social Security numbers, and dental or vision health information. Australian healthcare providers should take note of this incident, as it highlights the importance of robust cybersecurity measures in protecting sensitive patient information, in line with the requirements of the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires entities to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. This incident serves as a reminder for Australian healthcare organisations to review their cybersecurity protocols and ensure they are aligned with the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance to mitigate the risk of similar incidents. The fact that stolen data has been leaked online also underscores the need for proactive measures to prevent and respond to cyber threats, as outlined in the SOCI Act. Australian healthcare providers should consider offering similar support, such as credit monitoring and identity theft protection services, in the event of a data breach. By prioritising cybersecurity and being prepared to respond to incidents, Australian healthcare organisations can better protect sensitive patient information and maintain trust in the healthcare system. The incident at DentaQuest is a significant reminder of the ongoing threats to healthcare data and the need for vigilance in protecting this information. As the cybersecurity landscape continues to evolve, it is essential for Australian healthcare providers to stay informed and adapt their cybersecurity strategies to address emerging threats. This includes regularly reviewing and updating cybersecurity protocols, providing training to staff, and ensuring that incident response plans are in place. By taking a proactive approach to cybersecurity, Australian healthcare organisations can reduce the risk of data breaches and protect sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multi-factor authentication to prevent unauthorised access to systems and data, as recommended by the ASD Essential Eight
- Regularly review and update incident response plans to ensure they are aligned with ACSC guidance and the SOCI Act
- Provide training to staff on cybersecurity best practices and the importance of protecting sensitive patient information, in line with the Australian Privacy Principles
