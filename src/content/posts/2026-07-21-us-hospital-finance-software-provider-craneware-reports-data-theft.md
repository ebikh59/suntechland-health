A significant data theft incident has been reported by Craneware, a US hospital finance software provider, resulting in the exfiltration of a substantial volume of file names from its data environment. The company, which provides accounting and billing software to around 2000 hospitals and health systems in the US, confirmed that some employee data and a subset of customer and partner records were accessed and stolen. Although much of the data was non-sensitive or already publicly available regulatory data, the incident highlights the risks associated with cyber attacks on healthcare providers and their suppliers. For Australian healthcare providers, this incident serves as a reminder of the importance of protecting sensitive information in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires entities to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. Australian healthcare organisations should review their cybersecurity measures, including implementing the ASD Essential Eight, to prevent similar incidents. The Craneware incident also underscores the need for healthcare providers to be vigilant when engaging with third-party suppliers, as these suppliers can be a target for cyber attackers seeking to access sensitive healthcare data. By prioritising cybersecurity and complying with relevant regulations, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of data breaches and protect sensitive patient information. The incident has prompted cybersecurity experts to commend Craneware on its swift response to contain the incident, while also expressing concerns about the ease with which determined attackers can carry out data exfiltration. As the healthcare sector continues to be a prime target for cyber attackers, Australian healthcare providers must remain vigilant and proactive in their cybersecurity efforts.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies to prevent cyber attacks, including patching applications and operating systems, and restricting administrative privileges
- Regularly review and update contracts with third-party suppliers to ensure they adhere to Australian cybersecurity standards and comply with the Privacy Act 1988
- Conduct thorough risk assessments and due diligence on third-party suppliers, including evaluating their cybersecurity measures and compliance with the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme
