A recent surge in ransomware attacks has been reported, with a 19% increase in July compared to June, heavily impacting the finance, tech, and healthcare industries. According to analysis by Comparitech, the total number of claimed ransomware attacks in July was 799, making it the second-highest month of 2026. This surge is particularly concerning for Australian healthcare providers, as they must comply with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, which mandate the protection of sensitive patient information. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires healthcare providers to notify the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner and affected individuals in the event of a data breach. The healthcare sector experienced a 46% increase in ransomware attacks, highlighting the need for robust cybersecurity measures to prevent and respond to such incidents. Australian healthcare providers should prioritise regular backups, as emphasized by Rebecca Moody, head of data research at Comparitech, to ensure they can reset systems and restore data quickly in the event of an attack. The ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance provide valuable resources for healthcare providers to strengthen their cybersecurity posture and protect against ransomware threats. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act highlights the importance of protecting critical infrastructure, including healthcare services, from cyber threats. As ransomware groups continue to evolve and target various sectors, Australian healthcare providers must remain vigilant and proactive in their cybersecurity efforts to prevent and respond to these threats.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement regular backups and backups of backups to ensure business continuity in the event of a ransomware attack
- Apply the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies to prevent ransomware attacks, including patching applications and operating systems, and restricting administrative privileges
- Follow ACSC guidance on ransomware protection and response, including having an incident response plan in place and conducting regular cybersecurity exercises to test preparedness
