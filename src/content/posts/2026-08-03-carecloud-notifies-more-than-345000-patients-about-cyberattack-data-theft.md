A significant cyberattack has resulted in the theft of sensitive data from over 345,000 patients, with the breach affecting a cloud-based electronic health record environment. The incident occurred between March 10 and March 16, 2026, when an unauthorised third party accessed the environment, with the threat actor claiming to have exfiltrated databases. The compromised data includes names, addresses, dates of birth, and medical and health insurance information, highlighting the importance of robust cybersecurity measures in protecting sensitive patient data. For Australian healthcare providers, this incident serves as a reminder of the need to ensure compliance with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, as well as adherence to the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight guidelines and ACSC guidance can provide valuable frameworks for mitigating the risk of similar incidents. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 also plays a crucial role in ensuring the security and integrity of healthcare infrastructure. As the Australian healthcare sector continues to adopt cloud-based solutions, it is essential that providers prioritise cybersecurity and implement robust measures to protect sensitive patient data. The incident also underscores the importance of incident response planning and notification procedures, as outlined in the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. By prioritising cybersecurity and complying with relevant regulations, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of similar incidents and protect sensitive patient data. The use of cloud-based services, such as those provided by CareCloud, can offer numerous benefits, but also introduces unique cybersecurity risks that must be carefully managed. Australian healthcare providers must remain vigilant and proactive in their approach to cybersecurity, leveraging guidance from the ACSC and other relevant authorities to ensure the security and integrity of their systems and data.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multi-factor authentication to prevent unauthorised access to cloud-based environments and systems
- Regularly update and patch software and systems to prevent exploitation of known vulnerabilities
- Conduct regular security audits and risk assessments to identify and mitigate potential security risks
