A significant cyberattack has forced AnMed, a nonprofit health system, to temporarily close 79 of its 106 facilities, resulting in disruption to patient services and postponement of scheduled appointments. The attack, which involved malware, has affected computer systems, phone lines, and internet connectivity. Although some facilities, such as AnMed Urgent Care locations and emergency rooms, remain open, the health system is currently unable to provide a timeline for when normal services will resume. This incident highlights the importance of robust cybersecurity measures for Australian healthcare providers, who must comply with the Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles, as well as the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. The ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance provide valuable recommendations for protecting against cyber threats, and Australian healthcare organisations should review their cybersecurity protocols to ensure they are adequately prepared to respond to similar incidents. In the event of a data breach, healthcare providers must notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, as required by the NDB scheme. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act (SOCI Act) imposes additional obligations on certain healthcare organisations to protect their critical infrastructure from cyber threats. By prioritising cybersecurity and implementing effective measures to prevent and respond to cyberattacks, Australian healthcare providers can minimize the risk of disruption to patient care and protect sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement regular software updates and patching to prevent exploitation of known vulnerabilities
- Use application whitelisting to restrict the execution of unapproved software and reduce the risk of malware infections
- Conduct regular cybersecurity risk assessments and penetration testing to identify and address potential weaknesses in IT systems
