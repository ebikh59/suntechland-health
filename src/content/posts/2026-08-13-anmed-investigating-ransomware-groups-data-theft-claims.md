A ransomware group known as The Gentlemen has claimed responsibility for a cyber attack on AnMed, with the group alleging it has stolen sensitive patient data, including HIV status, mental health information, and social security numbers. The attack has led to the closure of several AnMed facilities, although most have since reopened. Australian healthcare providers should be aware of the increasing threat posed by ransomware groups, which can have significant implications for the security of patient data. Under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, healthcare providers have a responsibility to protect patient data from unauthorised access or disclosure. In the event of a data breach, the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires providers to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner. The Australian Cyber Security Centre has issued guidance on how to prevent and respond to ransomware attacks, including the importance of implementing the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies. Australian healthcare providers should review their cybersecurity measures to ensure they are adequately protected against these types of threats. The use of multi-factor authentication, regular software updates, and employee education can help prevent ransomware attacks. In the event of an attack, having a robust incident response plan in place can help minimise the impact on patient care and data security. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act also highlights the need for healthcare providers to prioritise cybersecurity and protect against potential threats. As the threat landscape continues to evolve, it is essential for Australian healthcare providers to stay vigilant and proactive in their approach to cybersecurity.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement multi-factor authentication for all users accessing patient data and systems
- Regularly update and patch software and systems to prevent exploitation of known vulnerabilities
- Provide ongoing education and training to employees on cybersecurity best practices and the risks associated with ransomware attacks
