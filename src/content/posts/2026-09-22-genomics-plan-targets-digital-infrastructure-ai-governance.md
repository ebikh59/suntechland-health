## What happened

Australian governments will work towards nationally coordinated genomic data infrastructure and embed genomics into wider digital health, cyber security and artificial intelligence governance under a new five-year national framework, Pulse+IT reported.

The National Health Genomics Policy Framework and Implementation Plan 2026–2030 has been endorsed by Health Ministers and released by the Australian Government together with the states and territories. It sets out a coordinated national approach to making genomics a routine part of health care.

The consultation draft of the framework identified genomic samples, data and information management as one of its focus areas, and aimed to reduce fragmentation and unnecessary duplication of effort across jurisdictions.

The full detail of the cyber security and AI governance commitments is set out in the framework document published by the Department of Health, Disability and Ageing.

## Technical and operational significance

Genomic data is unlike most health data. It is:

- **Permanent.** A person cannot change their genome if it is exposed.
- **Familial.** It reveals information about relatives who never consented to testing.
- **Hard to de-identify.** Genomic sequences are inherently identifying, and re-identification techniques continue to improve.
- **Increasingly used for AI.** Large genomic datasets are valuable for research and AI model development.

Those characteristics make genomic data infrastructure a high-value target and a high-consequence breach risk. Moving from fragmented, jurisdiction-specific systems to coordinated national infrastructure could improve security consistency. It could also concentrate risk, so strong security architecture, access governance and monitoring are essential from the outset.

Explicitly linking genomics to cyber security and AI governance is significant. Genomic data should be covered by the same security standards and AI oversight as other sensitive health data, not managed as a separate research silo.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

Genomic testing is moving into mainstream care in Australia, including in oncology, rare disease, reproductive health and pharmacogenomics. Health services, pathology providers and research institutes that handle genomic data will need to meet the security and governance expectations emerging from the framework.

Practical issues include where genomic data is stored, who can access it for clinical versus research purposes, how consent for secondary use is recorded and honoured, and whether genomic datasets are used to train AI models. Privacy Act obligations for sensitive information apply. Some research may also be subject to additional ethics and data-sharing agreements.

## What to watch next

- Implementation milestones for national genomic data infrastructure.
- Security and governance standards developed for genomic data under the framework.
- How the framework interacts with privacy reform and AI regulation.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Identify where genomic data is held across clinical, laboratory and research systems, and classify it as highly sensitive.
- Apply strong access controls and audit logging to genomic datasets, separating clinical and research access.
- Record and enforce patient consent for secondary use of genomic data, including use in AI development.
- Include genomic data platforms in security risk assessments, penetration testing and breach response planning.
- Follow the framework's implementation plan and prepare for national security and governance standards for genomic data.
