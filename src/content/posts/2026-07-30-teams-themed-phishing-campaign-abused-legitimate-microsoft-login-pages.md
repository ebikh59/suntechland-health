A recent phishing campaign has been uncovered, which impersonated notifications from Microsoft Teams and exploited Microsoft's legitimate authentication infrastructure to compromise corporate Outlook, SharePoint, and OneDrive accounts. The campaign, which began in late June and continued into July, targeted users at 120 organisations, including those in the healthcare sector, with emails designed to appear as notifications from the company's HR department. By using legitimate Microsoft login pages, the attackers aimed to increase the chances of success by avoiding suspicious activity that users may be aware of. If a user clicked on the link in the message, they were taken to a legitimate OAuth authorise URL, where they were prompted to accept permissions, allowing the attacker to access the account and potentially exploit it for Business Email Compromise (BEC) attacks. This campaign highlights the importance of Australian healthcare providers being vigilant and taking steps to protect against such phishing attacks, particularly in light of the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB) scheme also requires organisations to notify individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) in the event of a data breach. Australian healthcare providers should be aware of the risks associated with phishing campaigns and take proactive measures to protect their systems and data, including implementing the ASD Essential Eight and following ACSC guidance. The Security of Critical Infrastructure (SOCI) Act also plays a crucial role in protecting critical infrastructure, including healthcare services, from cyber threats. By being aware of these phishing tactics and taking steps to educate users, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of a successful phishing attack and protect sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement user education and awareness programs to help identify and report suspicious emails and phishing attempts
- Configure systems to enforce multi-factor authentication and restrict access to sensitive data and systems
- Regularly review and update security controls, including implementing the ASD Essential Eight, to protect against phishing campaigns and other cyber threats
