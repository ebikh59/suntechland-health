## What happened

The UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) has decided not to pursue criminal prosecution of a former healthcare worker who tried to access and sell the medical records of the Princess of Wales. Instead, it issued a formal caution.

The ICO began a criminal investigation into an insider at the London Clinic in 2024, after reports emerged following the Princess's admission there for abdominal surgery. The nurse is believed to have been struck off.

In a statement on 17 June, the ICO said it had issued "a now former healthcare professional from London with a formal caution in relation to an offence under section 170(5) of the Data Protection Act 2018". It said the conduct "involved the deliberate misuse of highly sensitive personal information and an offer to disclose it for financial gain, representing a clear breach of trust", and that a caution was the "appropriate and proportionate enforcement response".

The ICO also considered whether there were wider organisational failings at the clinic, but decided they did not meet the threshold for enforcement action.

"People should be able to trust that the personal information they're giving to healthcare settings is safe and protected from exploitation," said Ian Hulme, the ICO's executive director for regulatory supervision. "We will not hesitate to pursue criminal prosecution where it is necessary and proportionate to do so."

## Technical and operational significance

Insider misuse is one of the hardest health data risks to prevent, because the person involved usually has legitimate access to clinical systems. The issue is not whether they can open a record but whether they have a care-related reason to.

Medical information is highly sensitive and can be sold, which is why it is treated as "special category" data under GDPR. The source notes earlier cases, including an NHS worker who pleaded guilty in 2010 to illegally accessing patient records. It also cites research suggesting insider threats are rising: one 2021 report found 35% of healthcare organisations had suffered cloud data theft by malicious insiders, and a more recent study found 42% of organisations had seen an increase in insider threats.

High-profile patients concentrate the risk. Celebrities, politicians, staff members and their families attract curiosity and commercial interest, and their records are more likely to be targeted.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

Australian health services face the same problem. Unauthorised access to a patient's record by an employee can be an eligible data breach under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme if it is likely to result in serious harm, and the My Health Records Act 2012 contains penalties for unauthorised collection, use or disclosure of My Health Record information.

The case also shows that regulators look at the organisation, not just the individual. Here the ICO concluded the clinic's failings did not warrant action. An Australian health service in a similar position would want to show it had role-based access, access auditing, staff training and a clear disciplinary response in place.

Practical controls are well established. They include "break the glass" warnings and justification prompts for sensitive records, proactive auditing of access to VIP and staff records, and alerts when someone views a record without a care relationship.

## What to watch next

- Whether the ICO's choice of a caution rather than prosecution influences how other regulators handle health insider cases.
- Professional regulator outcomes, which in Australia would involve AHPRA and the relevant national board.
- Growing use of analytics to detect inappropriate record access in EMR platforms.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Enable "break the glass" controls and justification prompts for flagged sensitive records, including VIPs, staff and their families.
- Audit access to high-profile and sensitive records proactively, and alert on access without a recorded treatment relationship.
- Apply least-privilege, role-based access in clinical systems and review it whenever staff change roles or leave.
- Regularly remind staff that record access is logged and monitored, and of the professional and legal consequences of inappropriate access.
- Include insider misuse scenarios in breach response plans, including assessment under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme and notifying affected patients.
