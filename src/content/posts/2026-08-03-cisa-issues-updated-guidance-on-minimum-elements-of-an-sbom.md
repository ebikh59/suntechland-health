A joint guidance on the minimum elements of a Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) has been published by the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), National Security Agency (NSA), and 15 international cybersecurity authorities. This guidance is crucial for Australian healthcare providers as it enables them to better understand the makeup of their software components and supply chains, making more risk-informed decisions. An SBOM is a detailed list of software components, including open-source libraries and hidden dependencies, which is vital in identifying vulnerable or risky components before patches are released by vendors. By obtaining an SBOM from a software vendor, Australian healthcare providers can implement temporary solutions to protect against software supply chain attacks, ultimately safeguarding sensitive patient information in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB) scheme also necessitates that healthcare providers take proactive measures to prevent and respond to data breaches, and having an SBOM can facilitate this process. Furthermore, adhering to the ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance can help healthcare providers bolster their cybersecurity posture. The updated guidance includes additional data fields, updates to existing components, and minor updates to improve information quality, demonstrating the evolving nature of software security. As Australian healthcare providers continue to rely on complex software systems, prioritising software transparency and security is paramount. The Security of Critical Infrastructure (SOCI) Act also underscores the importance of securing critical infrastructure, including healthcare services, from cyber threats. By embracing the updated SBOM guidance, Australian healthcare providers can enhance their cybersecurity and protect sensitive information. The guidance applies to all software solutions, with additional requirements potentially necessary for certain types of software, such as AI-based software systems and software-as-a-service (SaaS) solutions in cloud environments. Australian healthcare providers must assess their software solutions to determine if any further efforts are required to improve software transparency and security. In doing so, they can ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of sensitive patient information, ultimately upholding their obligations under the Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement the updated SBOM guidance to enhance software transparency and security
- Conduct regular assessments of software solutions to identify potential vulnerabilities and risks
- Integrate SBOMs into existing cybersecurity frameworks, such as the ASD Essential Eight, to bolster overall cybersecurity posture
