## What happened

St Vincent's has established a national AI Clinical Council to guide the adoption and governance of artificial intelligence across its hospital and aged care services. The council held its first meetings on 8 and 9 October 2026, according to Cyber Daily.

The council brings together clinical leaders from across the organisation. It will advise on identifying, evaluating and scaling AI technologies, with a focus on patient care, clinician wellbeing and safe adoption, Pulse+IT reported. St Vincent's operates 12 public and private hospitals and 25 aged care facilities.

According to Healthcare IT News, the council is multidisciplinary. It covers areas including clinical quality and safety, ethics, compliance, digital, data and governance. It provides practical advice on AI-enabled workflows and models of care but does not replace existing clinical, governance or approval processes.

A post from the launch said the council was established so that AI decisions draw on frontline clinical expertise and the views of those delivering care.

## Technical and operational significance

Many health organisations have struggled with where AI decisions should sit. If IT or digital teams own them, clinical risks may be underweighted. If individual departments choose tools independently, organisations end up with a patchwork of overlapping products, inconsistent data practices and unmanaged risk.

A council that sits alongside existing governance, rather than replacing it, can provide:

- **A consistent view** of which AI tools are being considered or used across sites.
- **Clinical input** into whether tools fit real workflows and improve care.
- **Early identification of risks**, including safety, privacy, security and equity.
- **Coordination with approval processes**, so promising tools are evaluated rather than adopted informally.

The breadth of membership matters. Including data, digital and compliance expertise alongside clinical leaders makes it more likely that privacy and security considerations are raised before tools are deployed.

## What it means for Australian healthcare

St Vincent's model is one that other Australian health and aged care providers could adapt. As national guidance, such as the ACSQHC's draft AI guidance for health services, places greater emphasis on organisational accountability, providers will need structures that connect clinical, digital and governance perspectives on AI.

For security and privacy leaders, a body like this is an opportunity to make sure AI tools go through security assessment, privacy impact assessment and vendor due diligence before scaling. They should seek a formal role in or alongside such councils.

## What to watch next

- The council's first recommendations and any AI deployments it supports across St Vincent's.
- Similar structures emerging in other health and aged care organisations.
- How the council aligns with national AI governance guidance and accreditation expectations.

## Recommended actions for Australian healthcare organisations

- Establish a multidisciplinary AI governance forum that includes clinical, digital, data, privacy, security, ethics and consumer perspectives.
- Make sure the forum feeds into existing clinical governance and approval processes rather than operating in parallel.
- Require every AI tool considered for adoption to complete security and privacy assessment before pilots involve real patient data.
- Maintain an organisation-wide register of AI tools in use and under evaluation, including those adopted by individual departments.
- Track post-deployment performance and incidents for AI tools, and report them to the governance forum.
