A recent report by the US Government Accountability Office has identified potentially duplicative cyber reporting requirements for critical infrastructure, which may have implications for Australian healthcare providers. The report found that out of 117 regulations across 9 critical infrastructure sectors, approximately 70% had the same kind of reporting requirement as another regulation, resulting in at least 125 total reporting requirements. This duplication can divert resources away from improving security and towards compliance activities. Australian healthcare providers, who are already subject to the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme under the Privacy Act 1988, may face similar challenges in managing multiple reporting requirements. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight guidelines provide a framework for healthcare providers to prioritise their cybersecurity efforts, but duplicative reporting requirements could hinder their ability to do so. As the Australian healthcare sector continues to rely on computer-based information systems, it is essential to streamline cybersecurity regulations and reduce the administrative burden on providers. The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 also plays a crucial role in regulating the security of critical infrastructure, including healthcare. By harmonising cybersecurity regulations and reducing compliance burdens, Australian healthcare providers can focus on improving their cybersecurity posture and protecting sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Review existing cybersecurity reporting requirements to identify potential duplication and streamline processes
- Implement the ASD Essential Eight guidelines to prioritise cybersecurity efforts and reduce the risk of cyber incidents
- Engage with industry peers and regulatory bodies to provide feedback on cybersecurity regulations and advocate for harmonisation and reduction of compliance burdens
