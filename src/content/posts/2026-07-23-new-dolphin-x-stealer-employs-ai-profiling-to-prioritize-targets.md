A newly discovered Windows infostealer and remote access trojan, known as Dolphin X, has been found to utilise an AI-powered profiling system to help cybercriminals identify their most valuable targets. This malware is designed to target over 300 applications and steal a wide range of sensitive data, including cryptocurrency wallets and cloud tokens. The AI Profiler feature automatically ranks infected users based on factors such as application usage and browsing activity, allowing attackers to quickly identify those most likely to provide valuable access or data. Australian healthcare providers must be aware of this threat, as the theft of sensitive data can have serious implications under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires organisations to notify individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner if a data breach is likely to result in serious harm. To protect against such threats, healthcare providers should focus on implementing robust security measures, including the ASD Essential Eight, and follow ACSC guidance on protecting against malware and cyber threats. The use of AI-powered profiling by Dolphin X highlights the need for organisations to stay vigilant and adapt their security measures to combat evolving cyber threats. Under the SOCI Act, organisations must also ensure they are taking reasonable steps to protect sensitive information. By prioritising security and implementing effective measures, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of a data breach and protect sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies to prevent malware infections, including patching applications and restricting administrative privileges
- Focus on detecting and responding to malicious behaviour, rather than relying solely on file signatures, to identify potential security threats
- Regularly review and update security protocols to ensure they are aligned with ACSC guidance and the Australian Privacy Principles, to protect sensitive patient information
