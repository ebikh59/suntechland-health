A recent cyberattack on Amgen, a biopharmaceutical company, has resulted in a data breach involving patient data, highlighting the ongoing threat to healthcare organisations worldwide, including those in Australia. The breach, which occurred due to unauthorised access to third-party-hosted cloud storage systems, has led to the exfiltration of proprietary data, patients' protected health information, and other sensitive data. Australian healthcare providers should be aware of the potential risks associated with cloud storage and the importance of implementing robust cybersecurity measures to protect sensitive patient data. Under the Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles, healthcare organisations have a responsibility to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal information, including health information. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires organisations to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight provides a framework for organisations to protect themselves against cyber threats, and healthcare providers should consider implementing these measures to mitigate the risk of a cyberattack. The Secure Online Communication and Information (SOCI) Act also provides guidance on the secure handling of sensitive information. In light of this breach, Australian healthcare providers should review their cybersecurity protocols and ensure they are taking adequate steps to protect patient data. This includes regularly reviewing and updating cloud storage security, implementing robust access controls, and ensuring that all staff are trained in cybersecurity best practices. By taking proactive steps to protect patient data, healthcare providers can reduce the risk of a cyberattack and ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of sensitive information. The Australian healthcare sector should remain vigilant and proactive in protecting against cyber threats, given the potential consequences of a data breach, including reputational damage, financial loss, and harm to patients.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Review and update cloud storage security protocols to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of sensitive patient data
- Implement robust access controls, including multi-factor authentication, to prevent unauthorised access to cloud storage systems
- Provide regular cybersecurity training to all staff, including training on the Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight and the Secure Online Communication and Information (SOCI) Act
