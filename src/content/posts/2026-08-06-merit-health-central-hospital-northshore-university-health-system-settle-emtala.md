A recent settlement in the United States has highlighted the importance of compliance with emergency medical treatment laws for healthcare providers. The Merit Health Central Hospital and NorthShore University Health System have agreed to settle alleged violations of the Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act (EMTALA), with Merit Health Central Hospital paying a $350,000 financial penalty. The EMTALA requires hospitals to provide a medical screening examination and stabilising treatment to patients presenting to their emergency department, regardless of their ability to pay. In this case, the hospital failed to provide adequate medical screening examinations and stabilising treatment to 14 patients between 2013 and 2015. While this settlement is in the US, Australian healthcare providers can learn from this example, particularly in regards to their own obligations under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Australian healthcare providers must ensure they are providing appropriate emergency medical treatment and handling patient data in accordance with the Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB) scheme. The Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC) provides guidance on protecting patient data, and healthcare providers should be aware of the Security of Critical Infrastructure (SOCI) Act and its implications for their sector. By prioritising patient care and data security, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of similar breaches and settlements. The ASD Essential Eight provides a framework for healthcare providers to protect their systems and data from cyber threats, and implementing these measures can help prevent breaches and ensure compliance with relevant laws and regulations. Australian healthcare providers should review their emergency treatment protocols and data handling practices to ensure they are meeting their obligations and providing the best possible care for their patients. This includes having robust systems in place for managing patient data and ensuring that all staff are trained in handling sensitive information. By taking a proactive approach to emergency medical treatment and data security, Australian healthcare providers can protect their patients and their organisations from potential breaches and settlements.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Review and update emergency treatment protocols to ensure compliance with relevant laws and regulations
- Implement the ASD Essential Eight to protect systems and data from cyber threats
- Provide training to staff on handling sensitive patient information and ensuring compliance with the Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB) scheme
