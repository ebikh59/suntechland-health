A series of data breaches have been announced by five healthcare entities in the United States, highlighting the ongoing threat of cyber attacks to the healthcare sector. The breaches, which occurred at the Women’s Center for Radiology in Florida, Optalis Management Solutions in Michigan, the Association for Neurologically Impaired Brain Injured in New York, the Cardiovascular Institute of New England in Rhode Island, and the Kubota Tractor Corporation in Texas, resulted in the exposure of sensitive patient information, including names, addresses, dates of birth, and health insurance information. These breaches serve as a reminder to Australian healthcare providers of the importance of implementing robust cybersecurity measures to protect patient data, as required by the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme also requires entities to notify affected individuals and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner in the event of a data breach. Australian healthcare providers should take note of the measures taken by the affected entities, such as offering complimentary credit monitoring and identity theft protection services to affected individuals, and review their own data privacy and security policies, procedures, and processes to reduce the likelihood of similar incidents. The ASD Essential Eight and ACSC guidance provide a framework for healthcare providers to follow in order to protect their systems and data from cyber threats. Furthermore, the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act (SOCI Act) also plays a crucial role in protecting Australia's critical infrastructure, including healthcare, from cyber threats. By prioritising cybersecurity and following these guidelines, Australian healthcare providers can reduce the risk of data breaches and protect sensitive patient information.

## What Australian Healthcare Organisations Should Do

- Implement the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies to prevent cyber attacks
- Regularly review and update data privacy and security policies, procedures, and processes to ensure compliance with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles
- Conduct regular security audits and risk assessments to identify vulnerabilities and take proactive measures to address them, in line with ACSC guidance
